BeitEnactedbytheSenateandHouseofRepresentativesinGeneralCourtconvened:
1 1 Purpose.
2 I. The frequency of significant weather events that result in flooding and other damage
3 across our state has increased and will continue to do so. Changes in our climate and weather
4 patterns are supercharging the water cycle, leading to heavier rainfall extremes across the country,
5 including in New Hampshire. A recent study from Dartmouth College found that events that will
6 bring 1.5 inches or more of heavy rainfall or melted snowfall in one day are anticipated to increase
7 52 percent by the end of the century. A report prepared by the National Oceanic and Atmospheric
8 Administration (NOAA) Office for Coastal Management for the Seabrook-Hamptons Estuary
9 Alliance (SHEA) in May 2024 concluded that there is 67 percent probability that sea levels will rise
10 between0.3and0.7feetby2030and0.5and1.3feetby2050.
11 II. The cost to address this challenge and make ourselves more resilient is enormous and
12 governments cannot be expected to provide all the necessary funding; it will take a public/private
13 partnershipandsomecreativethinking.
14 III. Much of the work that is taking place in our cities and towns to address this real risk is
15 focusedonimprovingtheresilienceofourpublicinfrastructure. Thatworkneedstocontinuebutwe
16 also need to provide our cities and towns with some additional tools in their toolbox to help
17 individual homeowners who are interested and willing to invest in making their properties more
18 resilienttoflooding.
