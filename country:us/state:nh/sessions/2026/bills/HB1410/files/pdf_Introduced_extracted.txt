ANALYSIS
This bill defines out-of-class electric vehicles, establishes disclosure requirements for sellers,
restricts public operation unless reclassified, sets equipment and licensing standards, imposes age
andsafetyrequirements,andestablishespenaltiesforviolations.

METHODOLOGY:
Thisbillcreatesanewclassificationforcertainhigh-powered,non-standardelectricvehiclesthat
fall outside of existing definitions as out-of-class electric vehicles (OCEVs). In addition to
definitions, it also establishes disclosure requirements, operating rules, safety standards, age
restrictions, and penalties. OCEVs are defined as motor vehicles solely for the purposes of DUI
statutes and are subject to all DUI conviction requirements and sanctions, as well as fines
associated with violations under the outlined legislation proposed. To implement these changes,
the Department of Safety states it would incur a one-time cost (information technology and form
changes)of$16,000inFY2027.

This bill adds, deletes, or modifies a criminal penalty, or changes statute to which there is a
penalty for violation. Therefore, this bill may have an impact on the judicial and correctional
systems, which could affect prosecution, incarceration, probation, and parole costs, for the state,
as well as county and local governments. A summary of such costs can be found at:
https://gencourt.state.nh.us/lba/Budget/Fiscal_Notes/JudicialCorrectionalCosts.pdf
