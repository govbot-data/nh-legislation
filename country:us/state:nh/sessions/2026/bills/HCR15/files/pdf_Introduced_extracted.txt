1 Whereas, the United States Information and Educational Exchange Act of 1948, known as the
2 Smith-Mundt Act, was designed to broadcast information about the United States to foreign
3 audiencesasaformof"publicdiplomacy".
4 Whereas, the Smith-Mundt Act explicitly prohibited the Department of State and the
5 Broadcasting Board of Governors from disseminating material domestically out of concern for
6 creatinggovernment-fundedpropaganda.
7 Whereas, the Smith-Mundt Modernization Act of 2012 lifted the domestic distribution ban,
8 allowing government-produced foreign communication to be made available inside the United States
9 uponrequest.
10 Whereas, this change allowed government-produced information to be more widely disseminated
11 within the United States for the first time since the original act passed, leading to concerns that it
12 couldbeusedtoinfluencepublicopinion.
13 Whereas, despite the repeal of the dissemination ban, restrictions against directly targeting
14 domestic audiences remain in place, and United States law technically prohibits funds from being
15 used"toinfluencepublicopinionintheUnitedStates".
16 Whereas, the rise of the Internet and global broadcasting has made these distinctions difficult to
17 enforce, as content intended for foreign audiences can be easily accessed online within the United
18 States.
19 Whereas, the Smith-Mundt Modernization Act of 2012 has contributed to the media's ability to
20 spreadmisinformationandpropagandatoinfluencepublicopinion.
21 Whereas, the media's ability to spread misinformation and propaganda was exemplified by the

26 misinformationaboutCharlieKirk.
