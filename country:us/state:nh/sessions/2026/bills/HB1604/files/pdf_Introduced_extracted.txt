1 1 New Section; Use of Public Buildings as Polling Places. Amend RSA 658 by inserting after
2 section9-athefollowingnewsection:
3 658:9-b UseofPublicBuildingsasPollingPlaces.
4 I. Upon request by the selectmen of a town or the ward officers of a city ward, any town- or
5 city-owned facility within the town or ward, including schools, town halls, fire stations, or other
6 municipal or school district property, that is of sufficient size and accessibility to meet the
7 requirements of RSA 658:9, shall be made available for use as a polling place for all elections,
8 includingstate,federal,andmunicipalelections.
9 II. Whendesignated as apollingplace, suchfacilityshall bereserved as necessaryto ensure
10 the conduct of the election and the free and convenient access of voters, election officials, and
11 equipment, while permitting the continuation of normal governmental or educational operations,
12 includingemergencyresponseandpublicsafetyservices.
13 III. The municipality shall be responsible for reasonable costs directly related to such use,
14 includingcustodialservices,utilities,andsecurity,unlessotherwiseagreedbytheparties.
15 2 EffectiveDate. Thisactshalltakeeffect60daysafteritspassage.

METHODOLOGY:
This bill requires any suitable city or town owned facility within the town or ward, such as
schools, fire stations, and town halls, be made available as polling places upon request by the
selectmen of a town or the ward officers of a city ward. The municipality would be responsible
for reasonable costs associated with custodial services, utilities, and security unless otherwise
agreeduponbytherelevantpartiesinvolved.
The New Hampshire Municipal Association states adding an additional polling location could
increase local expenditures by approximately $10,000 to $100,000, primarily for staffing and
essential services. Furthermore, legal expenses may arise if disputes occur between facility and
municipalofficialsregardingwhatconstitutesreasonablecosts.
ItisassumedthatanyfiscalimpactwouldoccurafterFY2026.
