METHODOLOGY:
This bill proposes the establishment of a centralized, vendor-hosted Enterprise Resource
Planning (ERP) service for New Hampshire public school districts, aiming to modernize and
streamline administrative operations across finance, human resource, student information,
procurement,andrelatedsystems,whilepreservinglocalcontrolanddataownership.
TheDepartmentofEducation(NHED)assumestheintentandrationaleofthisbillaregrounded
inthefindingthatschooldistrictscurrentlyoperatedisparateandoftenoutdatedsystems,which
lead to inefficiencies and increased costs. By contrast, a centralized ERP platform could
generate cost savings through economies of scale, improve data security and compliance
(includingFamilyEducationalRightsandPrivacyAct(FERPA)),supportstandardized reporting
and analytics, and enhance interoperabilityand overall administrativecapacity. Under this bill,
NHED and the Department of Information Technology (DoIT) would lead a statewide

procurement process for such a platform in a way that supports local needs while leveraging
centralizedinfrastructure.
The bill establishes a structured, phased implementation plan. By November 1, 2026, NHED
would be required to develop a competitive request for proposal (RFP) that details system
requirements, security standards, pricing models, and vendor qualifications, incorporating
stakeholder input through an advisory group and public comment sessions. By April 1, 2027,
NHED would be required to issue the final RFP and publish it on the NHED website and the
state procurement portal. Participation by school districts is entirely voluntary; districts may
optinat their discretion, andno mandateis imposed. Tosupport participation, NHED would be
required to provide technical assistance, sample contracts, and vendor evaluation tools. NHED
may also initiate a pilot program with up to six diverse districts to test implementation and
training before a broader rollout. Under the bill, districts would retain ownership of their data,
vendors would be required to meet strict data security and privacy standards, and contracts
would be required to explicitly prohibit data monetization. Finally, NHED would be required to
report to the legislature following RFP issuance and again after any contract awards, and would
be granted rulemaking authority, in consultation with DoIT, to implement and oversee the
program.
TheDepartments(NHEDandDoIT)statetwo(2)full-timemanagementlevelpositions(SOC11-
06) would be required to implement this bill. The costs for these positions is estimated to be
$244,000 in FY 2027, $244,000 in FY 2028, and $256,000 in FY 2029. In addition, it is assumed
$50,000 would be needed, per year, in additional budgeted funds to support the positions and
their required duties. While this bill does not establish personnel, it does include a general fund
appropriationtoNHEDof$250,000inFY2027.
Given that district participation would be voluntary and with no information on final contract
rates, the cost for participation bylocal districts is indeterminable. Dependingupon district size
and level of enterprise software, NHED states estimated local costs could range anywhere
between$50,000and$500,000,ormore.
