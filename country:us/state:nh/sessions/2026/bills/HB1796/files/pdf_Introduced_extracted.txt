ANALYSIS
This bill allows the use of psilocybin in approved clinical settings to treat qualified medical
conditions. The bill establishes a medical psilocybin program and regulatory board in the
departmentofhealthandhumanservicesanddirectsthedepartmenttolicensequalifiedproviders.

METHODOLOGY:
This bill establishes a controlled program for the medical use of psilocybin, overseen by a
regulatory board within the Department of Health and Human Services. The purpose is to
permitregulatedtherapeuticuseofpsilocybinforconditionsincludingmajortreatment-resistant
depression, Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD), substance use disorders, end-of-life care,
and other qualified conditions as approved. The bill sets up a licensing system for cultivation,
testing, and therapy providers, plus regulatory requirements for training, data collection,
reporting, and continuing education. The Department would be required to study additional
psychedelic compounds for possible therapeutic use, with annual reports on efficacy, outreach,
and adverse events mandated, along with confidentiality protections. The program would be
funded by licensing fees for providers, cultivation facilities, and testing laboratories, as well as
possible grants and other revenues specifically generated by the program’s activities. Licensing
fees from initial application, renewal, and operational changes would be deposited directly into
the therapeutic psilocybin treatment fund, creating a non-lapsing funding stream for oversight,
annualassessments,andexpansionactivities.
The Department assumes that it will need $1 million to $1.25 million in upfront costs to develop
anITsystemtomanagetheprogram. TheDepartmentfurtheranticipatesneedingthefollowing
fivenewpositions:

· One general operations manager at a cost of $71,000 in FY27, $146,000 in FY28, and
$153,000inFY29;
