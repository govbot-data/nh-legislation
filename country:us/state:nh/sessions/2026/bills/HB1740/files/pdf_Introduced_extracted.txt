ANALYSIS
This bill revises the standards and processes for the issuance of orders of protection in domestic
violence, law enforcement response, and relationship to criminal proceedings. This bill further
establishesadomesticviolencelawenforcementtrainingandsupportfund.

METHODOLOGY:
This bill repeals and reenacts RSA 173-B, making extensive changes to the standards,
procedures, and timelines applicableto domestic violence protective orders, including mandatory
law enforcement response, arrest standards, prerequisites for relief, new timelines for hearings,
required notices, and updated definitions. The bill also transfers jurisdiction over all civil
domestic violence protective order petitions to the Superior Court, while related domestic

relations matters continue in the Circuit Court. In addition, the bill establishes a new Law
Enforcement Training Fund to support training programs related to domestic violence
investigations and requires the Attorney General to appoint a law enforcement coordinator to
administerthefund.
The Judicial Branch states that implementation of this bill would require substantial
administrative work prior to the January 1, 2027 effective date, including redesign of all
domestic-violence forms and orders, updates to case-processing manuals and templates,
modifications to the case management system, reconfiguration of workflows between the Circuit
and Superior Courts, and statewide training for judges, clerks, and crisis-center partners. The
Branch notes that these tasks require project management, legal review, training development,
communication planning, and technical updates that cannot be absorbed within current
resources. To prepare for the effective date, the Branch estimates expenditures of more than
$100,000andlessthan$200,000inFY2027forpre-implementationwork.Afterimplementation,
ongoing system maintenance, training updates, and support would result in indeterminable
annualcosts,butlikelylessthan$10,000peryear.
The Department of Justice(DOJ) states thebill would require theAttorneyGeneral toappoint a
law enforcement coordinator responsible for administering the new Law Enforcement Training
Fund, evaluating domestic-violence training curriculum, and supporting local law enforcement.
The DOJ notes that the position must include both programmatic expertise and financial
management skills, and because the bill does not specify the level or classification of the
coordinator, the Department cannot estimate salary and benefit cost at this time. The
Department states the position and associated administrative responsibilities will result in an
indeterminable increase in state General Fund expenditures between 100,000 to $500,000 per
fiscal year. The DOJ also states the bill does not provide sufficient funding to support these
responsibilities and that its existing budget cannot absorb costs. With respect to the Law
Enforcement Training Fund, DOJ states that because the bill does not identify a revenue source
or specify how much money will be deposited into the fund, state revenues are indeterminable,
but could be between $10,000 to $100,000 per year, depending on future legislative
appropriations,grants,orotherfundingstreams.
The Department of Safety states this bill would increase state expenditures due to expanded
mandatory law enforcement response requirements, investigative standards, arrest timelines,
and reporting obligations related to domestic violence incidents. The bill requires law
enforcement agencies to perform specific investigative tasks in all reported cases, forward
investigative reports within certain timeframes, and obtain arrest warrants in circumstances
previously permitted without a warrant. The Department states these requirements would

significantly increase staff time, overtime, and training needs. As a result, the Department
estimates increased expenditures of more than $100,000 and less than $500,000 per fiscal year
withhalftheimpactbeginninginFY2027andthefullimpactbeingfeltinFY2028andforward.
Theseexpenditureswouldbesplit75%GeneralFundand25%HighwayFund.
The Police Standards and Training Council states this bill would require a complete rewrite of
academy curriculum, scenario training, testing materials, and legal instruction related to
domestic violence response and arrest authority. As currently written, the bill does not clearly
state whether the Council would be eligible to access the newly established Law Enforcement
Training and Support Fund. If the bill is amended to allow the Council to use the fund, there
would be no additional fiscal impact to the Council’s operating budget. If not, the fiscal impact
would be indeterminable due to curriculum redevelopment costs and the unknown scope of
requiredstatewideorregionaltraining.
The New Hampshire Municipal Association (NHMA) states there will likely be increased local
expenditures associated with enforcing protective orders, including service of process, data
reporting, follow up compliance checks, training, and other activities required under the bill.
The NHMA notes these costs may be offset in future years by reimbursements from the Law
EnforcementTrainingandSupportFund. Anyadditionalmunicipalexpenditureswilldependon
the number of reports alleging abuse under RSA 173 B:1, I within a community and the results
oftherelatedinvestigations.
