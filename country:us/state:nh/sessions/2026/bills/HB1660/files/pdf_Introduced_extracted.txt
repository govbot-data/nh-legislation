1 1 Purpose. Thegeneralcourtherebyfindsthat:
2 I. NewHampshiremunicipalitiescurrentlyhaveauthorityunderRSA162-Ktoestablishtax
3 incrementfinancing(TIF)districts.
4 II. TIF districts allow a municipality to capture increases in property tax value within a
5 designateddistrictanddirectthoserevenuestowardpublicimprovements.
6 III. Municipalities may also negotiate agreements with developers under a TIF framework,
7 buttheseagreementsaretiedtodistrictformationandprimarilyusedforcommercialdevelopment.
8 IV. Credit enhancement agreements (CEAs) provide communities with a tool to address an
9 urgent need by incentivizing specific projects, tailoring requirements to local circumstances, and
10 streamliningtheprocesswithoutrequiringaTIFdistrictdesignation.
11 V. New Hampshire faces a shortage of senior housing, skilled care facilities, and workforce
12 housing.
13 VI. Current TIF law is not well-suited to housing because district formation is burdensome
14 andpoliticallycontentious,particularlyforsmall-scaleprojects.
15 VII. Without explicit statutory authority, municipalities lack a flexible tool to encourage
16 housingconstructionoradaptivereuseprojects.
17 VIII. Including housing-related captured assessed value in equalized valuation penalizes
18 townsbyartificiallyinflatingtheirstatepropertytaxbase,disincentivizinguseofTIFforhousing.
