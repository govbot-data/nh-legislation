METHODOLOGY:
This bill expands on the existing granite state paid family leave plan with a new publicly
administered family and medical leave insurance (FMLI) program. All approved PFML claims
costs and administrative expenses would be funded by employee payroll contributions. The bill
directs the State Board of Investment to set contribution rates sufficient to cover 135% of
benefits paid and 100% of administrative costs. The maximum duration of benefits would be 26

weeks for an employee’s own illness and 12 weeks for the illness of a family member. The
Insurance Department is designated as the primaryadministrative authority for the Family and
Medical Leave Insurance (FMLI) program. The Department is responsible for establishing and
managing the program, including collecting payroll contributions, determining eligibility and
benefit claims, administering appeals, and ensuring confidentiality of personal and medical
information. It must also oversee public education efforts funded by 5% of annual collections
from the FMLI fund. The Department shall adopt rules necessary for implementation, to
managetheFMLIfund,andreportannuallyonprogramperformanceandutilization.
The Insurance Department states this bill would result in an indeterminable impact to state
revenues and state expenditures. The bill establishes a state administered paid family and
medical leave insurance program funded through employer payroll contributions, with the
Insurance Department responsible for program administration, compliance monitoring, and
enforcement. The Department states revenues generated from employer contributions would be
deposited into a dedicated Paid Family and Medical Leave Insurance Fund and may be used to
offsetsomeadministrativecosts;however,thecontributionratehasnotyetbeenestablishedand
revenuescannotbeestimated.
The Department further states implementation of the program would require significant start-
up and ongoing expenditures, including rulemaking, payroll reporting, claims processing, data
privacy and cybersecurity, information technology systems, appeals administration, public
education and outreach, and ongoing compliance and enforcement activities. The bill does not
provide funding for these initial implementation costs, and a feasibility study or similar
assessment would be required to determine the technology, infrastructure, and staffing needed
to administer the program. Because the scope, timing, and cost of implementing the program
cannot be determined at this time, the Department estimates the fiscal impact would be
indeterminablebutlikelymorethan$2.5million.
The Department of Administrative Services indicates that the bill does not provide startup
funding and assumes that a third-party carrier would be procured to administer claims, with
premiums covering the cost of administration. The bill also does not repeal the current PFML
program, which requires the state to pay premiums to provide benefits to eligible employees.
DAS estimates the total cost of the new program at $1.8 million, with $662,637 coming from
General Funds. If the proposed mandatory PFML replaces the current voluntary plan, the cost
of coverage could double to $3.7 million, including $1.3 million in General Funds, due to the
expansionofcoveragefrom6weeksto12weeks.
When implementing the voluntary PFML plan in fiscal year 2023, DAS budgeted $2 million in
GeneralFundstosupporthiringonefull-timestaffmember,aconsultantforactuarialservices,a

marketing firm, and a website development firm. Given that the proposed program would be
mandatory, ongoing education costs are expected to continue beyond initial implementation. In
total, the estimated annual cost for providing 12 weeks of PFML coverage to state employees is
$3.7 million. When combined with the $2 million in implementation costs, the total projected
annualexpenserisesto$5.7million.
Counties and municipalities may experience increased expenditures related to employer payroll
contributions, but participation costs are uncertain and dependent on future contribution rates
and employee use of the program. The fiscal impact to county and local governments is
indeterminable.
