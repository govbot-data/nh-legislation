4 COMFORTDOGCERTIFICATION
5 167-E:1 Definitions.
6 I. “Facilitycomfort dog” meansanagencyowned dogthatprovides support tothepublic and
7 has met thecertificationrequirements of this chapter. Afacilitycomfort dogis aspecifically trained
8 dog that provides emotional support, comfort, and crisis response to individuals, groups, and
9 communities in various settings, such as hospitals, nursing homes, schools, disaster relief areas,
10 emergency response/public safety agencies, and other places where people may be experiencing
11 stress, trauma, or crisis. Facility comfort dogs are not considered service dogs or emotional support
12 animalsanddonothavethesamerights.
13 II. “Facilitycomfortdoghandler”or“handler”meansanemergencyresponseorpublicsafety
14 worker who is responsible for the care, training, and supervision of a facility comfort dog during its
15 interactions with individuals, groups, and communities in need of emotional support or crisis
16 response. A handler facilitates the facility comfort dog's work by ensuring that it behaves
17 appropriately, follows commands, and maintains a calm and comforting presence in various
18 environments. A handler is trained to understand canine behavior, communication, and emotional
19 support techniques, allowing them to effectively guide the facility comfort dog's interactions with
20 individuals, groups, or communities in distress or crisis situations. A handler maintains the well-
21 being and safety of both the facility comfort dog and the individuals, groups, and communities they
22 serve.
23 III. “Facility comfort dog trainer” or “trainer” means a person retained to train the facility
24 comfort dog and handler for the work the facility comfort dog will do within public safety or
25 emergency response. A trainer has a minimum of 5 years experience with focused training of
26 assistancedogs. Genericpettrainingshallnotqualifyasexperience.
27 IV. “Facility comfort dog team” means a trained facility comfort dog and its handler,
28 working collaboratively to provide emotional support, comfort, and crisis response to individuals,
29 groups,andcommunitiesinvarioussettings.
