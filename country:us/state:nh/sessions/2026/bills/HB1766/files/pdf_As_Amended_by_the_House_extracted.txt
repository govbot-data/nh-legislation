METHODOLOGY:
This bill establishes procedures for the confiscation and care of livestock in animal cruelty cases,
includingrequirementsforinvestigation,custody,hearings,anddispositionoflivestock.
The Department of Agriculture, Markets, and Food states this bill would require the State
Veterinarian’s office to respond to and investigate complaints involving livestock, including field
investigations, coordination with law enforcement, communication with animal owners, report
writing, and follow-up activities. The Department assumes there would be more than 50 calls
perweekandatleast50investigationsannually,eachrequiringmultipledaysofstafftime.
The Department states the work required by this bill cannot be absorbed within its existing
budget and would require additional staffing and resources. It anticipates needing one (1)
Animal Health Technician (SOC 19-4010; start date January 1, 2027) at an estimated cost of
$85,000 in FY 2027 (including vehicle and initial equipment costs), $84,000 in FY 2028, and

$87,000 in FY 2029. The Department also anticipates needing one (1) Veterinarian (start date
January 1, 2027) at an estimated cost of $80,500 in FY 2027 and $161,000 in each FY 2028 and
FY 2029. The total for both positions would be $165,500 in FY 2027, $245,000 in FY 2028, and
$248,000 in FY 2029. These positions are neither authorized nor funded in this bill. The
Departmentmayseekfundinginafuturebiennialbudgetrequest.
The Judicial Branch states this bill aligns with existing court processes for animal cruelty cases
andthereforedoesnotresultinanyadditionalfiscalimpacttotheBranch.
Lastly, the fiscal impact of this bill on counties and municipalities is indeterminable. Local law
enforcement agencies, animal control officers, and other entities may incur costs related to the
investigation, transportation, care, and custody of livestock; however, the extent of any such
impact will depend on the number and complexity of cases, the duration of custody, and the
applicabilityofcostrecovery.
