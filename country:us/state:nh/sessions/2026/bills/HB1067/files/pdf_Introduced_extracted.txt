METHODOLOGY:
This bill revises the statute governing the establishment and operation of mental health courts
and requires theJudicial Branchtocreateand administer agrant program tofund county-based
or nonprofit-operated mental health court programs. The bill directs the Office of Statewide
TreatmentCourts(OSTC)tocertify,oversee,andcollectdatafromparticipatingprograms.
The Judicial Branch states this bill will result in an increase in state expenditures from the
General Fund. The fiscal impact depends on how many counties apply for certification and the
levelofgrantfundingawarded.
If implemented statewide across all 11 county-based locations (including two in Hillsborough
County), total estimated expenditures are as follows: $2.0m in FY 2027, $3.8m in FY 2028 and
FY 2029. Funding is assumed to come primarily from the General Fund, with potential use of
approximately$250,000infederalorotherfundsduringthestart-upyear.

TheFY2027estimatereflectsapartial-yearimplementationinwhichtheJudicialBranchwould
establish the statewide Office of Statewide Treatment Courts, hire a coordinator, begin program
development, and provide limited start-up grants to several pilot counties. These pilot grants
are expected to range between $100,000 and $150,000 per site for initial case-management staff,
training,andtechnologysetup.
The FY 2028 and FY 2029 estimates assume full implementation of 11 mental health court
programs statewide. Each program is estimated to cost approximately $325,000 per year, which
includes:
· Judicial and case-management time (judge participation, program coordinator, and
casemanager)
· Participantassessment,treatmentcoordination,anddatacollection
· Training,certification,andoversightbytheOSTC
· Technology,evaluation,andreportingrequirements
These figures also include approximately $225,000 annually for statewide administrative
support,training,anddata-systemmaintenanceundertheOSTC.
Because the bill does not include an appropriation or identified funding source, the Judicial
BranchindicatestheprogramcouldnotbeimplementedwithoutanappropriationtotheJudicial
Branchtofundthenewcourts.
It is assumed the fiscal impact will begin in FY 2027, with any ongoing costs incorporated into
theBranch’sFY2028–FY2029budgetrequest.
