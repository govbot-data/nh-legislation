1 1 Findings. Thegeneralcourtfindsthat:
2 I. The release of post-arrest photographs (commonly known as mugshots) of individuals
3 accused but not convicted of crimes can cause substantial and lasting reputational harm, even when
4 theindividualisultimatelyacquittedorthechargesaredismissed.
5 II. The public dissemination of such photographs for nonviolent offenses before a conviction
6 serveslittlepublicsafetypurposeandcanunderminetrustinthecriminaljusticesystembycreating
7 apresumptionofguilt.
8 III. Limiting public access to post-arrest photographs for nonviolent offenses until after
9 conviction will help protect the privacy and due process rights of individuals while maintaining
10 publicconfidenceinlawenforcement.
11 IV. Exceptions are necessary to allow for the release of post-arrest photographs when there
12 is an urgent public safety need or when the assistance of the public is necessary to locate a suspect
13 whoposesadangertothecommunity.
14 V. These protections are not intended to apply to individuals determined to be unlawfully
15 present in the United States, for whom different public safety and immigration enforcement
16 considerationsapply.
