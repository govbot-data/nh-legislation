METHODOLOGY:
This bill adopts the Uniform Child Abduction Prevention Act. In so doing, it establishes court
procedures in which petitioners may request the issuance of orders imposing child abduction
prevention measures, and gives courts the ability under certain circumstances to issue warrants
authorizing law enforcement to take physical custody of children deemed at risk of abduction.
The Judicial Branch states that, in order to support judges in fulfilling their obligations under
the bill, it will need to employ an individual with expertise in immigration practices, the Hague
ConventionontheCivilAspectsofChildAbduction,domesticviolence,childprotection,theState
Department's Child Passport Issuance Alert Program, foreign consulate and embassy
procedures, and other highly specialized skills. The Branch also anticipates hiring a case
manager to handle the increased volume of filings and case processing requirements resulting
from the bill. Combined, the two positions are anticipated to cost approximately $173,000 per
year,withhighercostspossibleiftheworkloadisgreaterthananticipated.
The Department of Health and Human Services states that it has no data from which to
extrapolate the costs of the entirely new process for abduction prevention measures created by
thebill,andsothefiscalimpactisindeterminable.
