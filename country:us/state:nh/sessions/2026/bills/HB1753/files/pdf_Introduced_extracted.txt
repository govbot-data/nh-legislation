METHODOLOGY:

This bill establishes the New Hampshire First for Veterans Program and creates the New
Hampshire First for Veterans Authority as a public instrumentality of the state to provide
grants, block grants, and loans to support veterans, service members, and their families. The
bill creates two nonlapsing funds, the First for Veterans Administrative Fund and the New
HampshireFirstforVeteransTrustFund,andredirectsrevenuescollectedunderRSA261:75-97
into these new funds. The bill also provides that principal and interest repayments on loans
issued by the authority be deposited into the Land and Community Heritage Investment
ProgramAdministrativeFund.
The Department of Safety states this bill would result in a loss of revenues currently deposited
into existing motor vehicle plate related funds, including funds used to support license plate
production and associated operations. The Department estimates the loss of revenues to the
plate funds would be $4,815,840 in FY 2027, $4,966,316 in FY 2028, and $5,121,305 in FY 2029.
The Department states that under this bill these revenues would instead be credited to the First
for Veterans Administrative Fund and the New Hampshire First for Veterans Trust Fund and
disbursed in accordance with the bill. As a result, revenues to the newly established funds
would increase by corresponding amounts, while revenues to the funds that currently receive
RSA 261:75-97 proceeds, including the Reflectorized Plate Inventory Fund, the Conservation
Plate Fund and the Park Plate Fund, would decrease by the same amounts. The net impact on
state revenues is indeterminable because the bill does not provide replacement funding for the
affected plate related funds. The Department further notes that the redirection of these
revenues could impact the existing plate production operations absent an alternative funding
source.
The Department of Military Affairs and Veterans Services anticipates an increase in general
fund expenditures due to the need for a new permanent full-time position to manage the
program, with projected costs of $105,000 in FY 2027, $107,000 in FY 2028, and $112,000 in FY
2029. However, because the legislation does not define staffing levels, administrative structure,
program scope, or grant funding amounts, the full cost of operating the authority cannot be
determined.
Local expenditures and revenues may change only if municipalities or counties voluntarily apply
for and accept grants or loans from the authority. Because local participation is optional, and
thescopeofavailablegrantfundingisunknown,thelocalimpactisindeterminable.
